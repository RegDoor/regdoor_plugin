# Legal and regulatory quality

## Use this guidance selectively

Apply this workflow when the answer turns on the wording or structure of a specific legal or regulatory instrument. Do not apply it mechanically to conceptual comparisons, general policy explanations, or style-only rewrites.

## Build the provision map

Identify only the elements material to the requested conclusion:

1. the instrument and its legal or procedural nature;
2. the actor or class of actors governed by the rule;
3. the regulated activity, transaction, product, or conduct;
4. the operative provision and any amendment relationship visible in the available record;
5. the relevant date, version, commencement, transition, and present enforceability; and
6. the decision or practical consequence the reader needs to understand.

When the supplied record contains both an amending instrument and a base instrument, state their relationship and distinguish the amendment act from the provision as amended. Do not imply that future text is already operative.

When an operative provision incorporates a definition or rule from another instrument, inspect that controlling cross-reference before paraphrasing it. If the referenced text is unavailable or unnecessary to the requested conclusion, preserve the legal term and state that the instrument adopts the definition in the referenced regulation; do not supply a plausible general definition as though it were the incorporated legal definition.

For a non-specialist audience, treat an incorporated definition as necessary when it determines whether an actor, asset, destination, activity, or transaction falls within the rule. Retrieve and identify the controlling provision rather than leaving the material term unexplained.

Describe what each material instrument actually governs. Separate:

- the subject and legal nature of the instrument;
- the exact amendment or operative rule used in the answer; and
- the practical or analytical consequence drawn from that rule.

Do not present an amendment to an antifraud regime as though it were a comprehensive regulation of the broader sector merely because its scope reaches that sector.

## Express unresolved legal gaps impersonally

If a material gap remains after proportionate research, adjust the legal conclusion rather than narrating the research problem. State which substantive element is unconfirmed and how that limits the conclusion, such as an unverified controlling definition, current version, commencement rule, covered class, exception, or actor-specific fact.

Do not attribute the limitation to the user, prompt, supplied material, an excerpt, a confirmed record, an available source set, a located or retrieved document, RegDoor, a database, a research tool, an agent, or a retrieval failure. Do not write `gap in RegDoor`, `the user did not provide`, `the excerpt does not reproduce`, `the source set does not contain`, `the provision was not examined`, `the document located does not contain`, or equivalent process language. A suitable formulation is: `The covered class cannot be determined without the controlling definition in the base regulation.`

If the missing element prevents a categorical answer, state a conditional result tied to the unresolved legal fact. If it affects only a secondary point, omit or qualify that point without weakening supported conclusions.

## Parse the normative logic

For each material rule, determine:

- **subject:** who is governed;
- **modality:** must, must not, may, may only, is exempt, or is subject to supervisory action;
- **object:** what activity, transaction, asset, service, or decision is governed;
- **trigger:** what event activates the rule;
- **cumulative conditions:** which elements must coexist;
- **alternative conditions:** which independent branch is sufficient;
- **threshold or timing:** what value, period, deadline, or sequence applies;
- **exception or discretion:** what can displace or modify the default rule;
- **procedure and evidence:** what notice, analysis, decision, record, or documentation is required; and
- **consequence or authority:** what follows and which actor may impose it.

Do not describe a permission as an obligation, a conditioned power as an automatic consequence, a precautionary measure as a permanent deprivation, or an alternative condition as cumulative.

When a later duty arises only if an event occurs during performance of an earlier duty, do not describe the entire sequence as cumulative. State the standing duty first and then the event-triggered duty, for example: the institution must test the plan periodically and, if the test identifies a critical failure, must notify the authority.

## Draft at the right level

- Use inline `(i)`, `(ii)`, `(iii)`, and at most `(iv)` when two to four parallel legal elements belong in one analytical passage and separating them makes the legal logic easier to audit. Suitable uses include cumulative requirements, alternative triggers, successive duties, exceptions, and distinct supervisory powers.
- Introduce the legal relationship before enumerating. State, for example, that conditions are cumulative, triggers are alternative, or duties follow in sequence; then separate the elements with semicolons when they contain internal detail.
- Keep each item grammatically parallel and legally distinct. Do not use `(i)`, `(ii)`, and `(iii)` merely to decorate a list of background facts.
- Use bullets or a compact table when the reader must track more than four conditions, actors, steps, or consequences.
- Preserve article, paragraph, item, and sub-item identifiers supplied by the record when they materially support the conclusion, but use them as parenthetical legal locators rather than as the default grammatical subject.
- Explain the actor-specific consequence after the rule rather than substituting an operational summary for the legal mechanism.

## Use negation only for legal work

Prefer an affirmative statement of the rule, change, or scope. A negative formulation is useful when it prevents a probable legal error, such as confusing a communication with an authorization, a precautionary hold with permanent unavailability, a permission with an obligation, or a future rule with a presently enforceable duty.

Remove a negative sentence when the affirmative formulation already defines the object and the excluded proposition is neither likely nor material. For example, if the text already states that an amendment extends an antifraud regime to a new actor, a separate statement that it does not create a comprehensive sector regime adds value only when that broader interpretation is genuinely at issue.

Do not convert omitted base-regime context into a negative catalog. When only specific provisions are extended, say which duties apply; do not enumerate authorization, capital, governance, outsourcing, disclosure, or other excluded subjects unless a particular exclusion changes the requested conclusion.

Do not delete a negative qualification that preserves modality, timing, scope, or consequence. The objective is to remove contrastive filler, not legal boundaries.

## Place legal locators without breaking the prose

- State the proposition first and place its locator in parentheses at the closest unambiguous point: `The new duties commence on 1 January 2027 (art. 3º).`
- Place a locator immediately after a named rule when the rest of the sentence depends on that provision: `The new provision (art. 2º-B) creates a precautionary hold.`
- Keep the instrument name or hyperlink in the prose and the article reference in parentheses when that improves readability: `the adaptation period under Regulation X (art. 88, § 2º)`.
- Use the shortest locator that preserves correct attribution. When one identified instrument plainly governs the passage, use a compact form such as `(art. 2º, II)`. When multiple instruments, identical article numbers, an amendment relationship, or an unclear antecedent create doubt, identify the source inside the parenthesis, for example `(art. 2º, II, da Resolução BCB nº 142/2021)`.
- For an amended rule, attribute the operative provision to the base text and identify the amending act when that relationship matters. Explain the relationship once in the prose or locator; do not load every later parenthesis with both instruments after the attribution is established.
- Do not append the same article to every successive sentence. One locator may support a short, cohesive passage when all of its propositions derive from that provision, no competing legal basis intervenes, and the reader can identify the supported passage without guessing. Repeat the locator when the legal basis changes, a new paragraph separates a material claim, the claim needs independent verification, or omission would create ambiguity.
- For an inline enumeration derived from one article, place one consolidated locator after the final item, such as `(art. 10, caput e incisos I a III)`, rather than repeating `(art. 10)` and then citing each item separately. Apply the same approach to a short analytical cluster supported by successive paragraphs, for example `(art. 10, §§ 1º a 3º)`, when the correspondence remains evident. Do not use consolidation to drop a separate article that independently establishes status, commencement, scope, an exception, or another material rule.
- Group locators that jointly support one proposition: `(arts. 1º, 4º e 6º-A)`. Use separate parentheticals only when different instruments or provisions support distinct clauses and consolidation would obscure which rule supports which proposition.
- When several instruments are discussed, include enough information to prevent misattribution. Distinguish the provision in the base text from the article of the amending act and state the amendment relationship in words.
- Avoid dangling modifiers. Make clear whether the amending instrument introduced a definition, inserted a provision, or changed wording; do not write a construction that could imply that the regulated object itself was “introduced” by the amendment.
- Put sentence punctuation after the closing parenthesis, except when a semicolon or colon continues the same sentence after the supported clause.
- Run a final locator scan. In narrative prose, treat `inciso VII do artigo 2º`, `novo artigo 15-B`, and equivalent wording as locators that must be recast, for example: `the amended definition in the base text (art. 2º, VII)` and `the new operational provision in the base text (art. 15-B)`.

Do not start analytical prose with `Art.`, `O art.`, `Nos termos do art.`, or equivalent formulae when the sentence can lead with the legal proposition. This placement rule does not authorize moving or rewriting article markers inside a direct quotation, nor does it override a user-requested formal citation table or source list.

## Preserve proportionality

Do not force article-level treatment when the user asks for a conceptual explanation and no legal conclusion depends on exact wording. Do not expand a style-only rewrite beyond its supplied record. If the requested limit prevents responsible provision-level treatment, prioritize the core rule and state the specific omitted scope or ask for a broader format when necessary.

## Final legal-quality check

Confirm that the answer:

- preserves the subject, modality, trigger, cumulative or alternative logic, exception, procedure, and consequence material to the conclusion;
- attributes amended provisions, incorporated definitions, and supervisory powers to the correct instrument and source;
- uses the shortest unambiguous parenthetical locators, consolidating or repeating them only when traceability requires it;
- states what each cited provision governs before distinguishing a legal rule from the analytical consequence; and
- connects the mechanism to the affected actor without adding legal formality or background that does not change the answer.

# Output patterns

## Use an adaptive base structure

Select the smallest useful set of components:

1. core answer or issue;
2. necessary context;
3. classification or perimeter;
4. legal or procedural status;
5. mechanism and practical implication;
6. unresolved issue or limitation; and
7. source basis.

Do not use every component as a heading. A short answer may express several components in two or three paragraphs.

## Choose the delivery register

Select the register from the reader's intended use, not from the apparent simplicity of the format:

| Register | Primary use | Organizing principle | Language |
|---|---|---|---|
| Policy advice | Support a decision or course of action | Conclusion, legal basis, conditions, risk, and next step | Direct and decision-oriented |
| Regulatory report | Create a durable analytical record | Scope, status, rule, evidence, consequence, and limitations | Technical, systematic, and precise |
| Regulatory update | Explain a new development | Instrument, principal change, mechanism, timing, and affected actors | Compact and institutional |
| Newsletter or news item | Inform a broader professional audience | Development, practical significance, mechanism, and what follows | Accessible and concise |
| Comparison | Distinguish regimes or options | Comparison thesis, repeated dimensions, and operational difference | Parallel and explicit |

The register may change sentence length, terminology, emphasis, and structure. It must not change the supported status, scope, legal logic, qualifications, or conclusion.

## Policy advice or regulatory answer

Lead with the conclusion and practical consequence. Add only the context needed to understand it. Classify adjacent categories when they could change the result. State status, mechanism, uncertainty, and sources in the body or concise sections.

When a decision is requested, identify the recommended or available course of action, the legal condition on which it depends, and the principal implementation or diligence step. Do not convert uncertainty into a categorical recommendation.

## Regulatory report

Build a durable record of the issue, scope, applicable instruments, status, operative mechanism, affected actors, consequences, and material limitations. Use headings when the length benefits from navigation, but do not fragment a short report or repeat an executive conclusion in every section.

Prefer stable institutional terminology and enough provision-level traceability for later review. Executive accessibility may change the order of presentation, not the completeness of the material legal analysis.

## Regulatory update

Open with the instrument, principal change, and concrete operative mechanism in the smallest number of sentences that remains clear. Add commencement or compliance timing when it changes what an affected actor must do or when; do not add an act date, gazette publication date, research cutoff, or `published but not yet enforceable` sentence merely because that information is available. If an amendment both expands the perimeter and applies a specific hold, deadline, control, or procedure, name that mechanism in the opening rather than postponing it behind the amendment relationship. Do not precede the development with generic sector background. Introduce the base instrument only to explain the affected perimeter, rule, definition, or consequence; avoid cataloging unrelated or excluded controls.

State the minimum timing fact needed for the conclusion. A deferred commencement date ordinarily resolves present enforceability without a second sentence tied to the research cutoff. Mention publication separately only when publication itself starts effects, establishes validity, resolves chronology, or was requested.

Use this sequence when it improves clarity:

1. what the instrument actually governs and its legal nature;
2. the principal change and operative mechanism;
3. material commencement, compliance, transition, or procedural status, only when it affects the requested conclusion;
4. affected actors, activities, products, or users;
5. the material conditions, duties, faculties, exceptions, or supervisory powers;
6. a material boundary or unchanged element, only when it corrects a likely misunderstanding or affects the decision;
7. implementation or transition; and
8. practical implication and source basis.

Do not treat every announcement as an update with binding effect.

When two to four legal elements need to remain in one analytical passage, introduce their relationship and use inline `(i)`, `(ii)`, `(iii)`, and at most `(iv)`. Prefer this pattern for a compact rule decomposition; use bullets when the elements require separate operational treatment.

Keep article, paragraph, item, and sub-item references parenthetical in analytical prose. Lead with the development or rule, then attach the locator to the supported clause. Prefer `The amendment broadens the covered institutions (art. 1º)` to `Art. 1º broadens the covered institutions`. Name the instrument inside the parenthesis only when attribution would otherwise be uncertain, as in `(art. 2º, II, da Resolução BCB nº 142/2021)`. After the source is established, use compact locators and avoid repeating the same article after every sentence when one reference can clearly support the cohesive passage. For a compact enumeration or cluster drawn from one article, cite its relevant subdivisions once after the unit, such as `(art. 10, caput e incisos I a III)` or `(art. 10, §§ 1º a 3º)`. Keep links on the instrument name when possible, rather than turning the locator into the sentence's visual focus.

## Newsletter or news item

Identify the newsworthy development and its practical significance in the opening. Use shorter sentences and less specialized wording than a regulatory report, while preserving the same supported legal status, mechanism, material conditions, qualifications, timing, and actor-specific consequence.

Expand acronyms at first occurrence and explain a technical or legal term when a broader professional reader may not know it. Translate rather than delete necessary legal detail: a compact explanation may replace dense terminology, but it must not turn a conditional rule into a categorical statement or a proposal into binding law.

Use provision locators and links proportionately in the body when they support material claims. Avoid a citation-heavy lead when the same traceability can be provided fluently later. Do not manufacture urgency, novelty, conflict, or market impact merely to create a news angle.

## Comparison

State the comparison thesis first. Compare regulatory architecture, authority, regulated object, authorization path, status, and practical consequence. Use a table only when it makes repeated dimensions easier to compare.

End with the operational difference, not a restatement of the laws listed.

## Rewrite or review

Preserve the user's facts, intended meaning, audience, and requested format. Improve classification, status, mechanism, consequence, and source discipline without silently introducing new claims.

Preserve material legal propositions rather than every source sentence or the original paragraph order. Identify the decision-driving mechanism across the full source and place it where it is most useful, normally in the opening. Remove factual background, examples, negative contrasts, and control inventories that do not change scope, operation, status, consequence, or a supported qualification.

When useful, provide:

1. the revised text; and
2. a short note identifying unsupported or ambiguous claims that require verification.

Do not add the note when the user requests only clean copy and no material integrity issue remains.

## Keep execution context outside the finished work

Apply audience, length, format, paragraph count, and research instructions silently. Do not reproduce prompt language, cutoffs, UUIDs, file paths, tool names, repository names, database coverage, search mechanics, or retrieval failures in a title or body unless the information is expressly requested as part of the deliverable or materially necessary to evaluate the legal conclusion.

Correct an imprecise instrument name or status directly in the opening, but preserve substantive ambiguity when more than one real act fits after checking instrument type, date, subject, and status. State unresolved limitations as legal boundaries, not as a report about the request, excerpt, supplied record, source set, located material, unexamined provision, agent availability, or research process. When a methodology note is requested, identify the date and substantive source universe, such as official central-bank instruments and the relevant base regulation, without naming internal retrieval infrastructure.

## Create a title only when requested

Draft the title after the body so it names the principal change and consequence, preferring the concrete operative mechanism to a broader description of scope or purpose. Do not add a title when the user did not request one.

- For a newsletter or news item, state the development and its practical consequence in accessible language, without clickbait or unsupported urgency.
- For a regulatory report, identify the instrument, regime, or legal issue and its analytical object precisely.
- For policy advice, foreground the decision, permission, restriction, or implementation issue the advice resolves.
- For a comparison, identify the regimes or approaches and the dimension that matters to the conclusion.

Keep a title consistent with the body and free of research cutoffs, references to the request, internal identifiers, tools, databases, and execution metadata. Include a date, instrument number, or procedural label only when it materially distinguishes the subject or the user requests it. Avoid generic labels such as `Regulatory update` when a specific substantive title is possible.

## Choose formatting deliberately

- Use headings to reveal analysis, not to fragment short prose.
- Use bullets for obligations, affected actors, procedural steps, compliance paths, timelines, and source lists.
- Use inline numbering for conceptual or legal separation of two to four dimensions when the relationship among them is stated and the numbering improves comprehension.
- Use the shortest unambiguous parenthetical provision locators for legal traceability, identifying the instrument when needed and avoiding repetition that interrupts the analytical flow.
- Use tables for repeated-field comparisons, status trackers, classifications, thresholds, or actor-obligation mappings.
- Use timelines only when sequencing or legal status depends on dates; include the date, event, significance, and current status.
- Explain the takeaway before or after a table or timeline.

Avoid slide-like prose, decorative tables, generic or sensationalist titles, repetitive labels, formulaic openings, redundant negative contrasts, repeated status conclusions, and background inventories that do not affect the answer.
